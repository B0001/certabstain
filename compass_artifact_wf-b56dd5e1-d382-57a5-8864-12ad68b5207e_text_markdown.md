# Certified Error Bounds for Learned Dynamics Models, with Depth on Contact Dynamics (State of the Art, July 2026)

## TL;DR
- For a genuinely *deterministic* uniform bound |ĝ(x)−g(x)| ≤ ε on a learned contact-dynamics function, only three routes are real in 2026: (a) **NN interval/reachability verification of the discrepancy** ĝ−g against a trusted reference (α,β-CROWN / auto_LiRPA / CORA), (b) **deterministic RKHS / kernel-ridge bounds** (Maddalena–Scharnhorst–Jones; Fiedler–Scherer–Trimpe) assuming a known RKHS-norm bound and bounded noise, and (c) **Lipschitz interpolation / nonlinear set-membership** (Milanese–Novara; Calliess LACKI) assuming a known Lipschitz constant. Every route buys its ε with a *declared, non-trivial assumption* that is hard to defend for stiff contact.
- Contact breaks the assumptions all three routes rely on: stiffness makes the true one-step map *nearly discontinuous*, so global Lipschitz constants explode and both covering arguments and Lipschitz-growth rollout bounds become vacuous. The literature's own workaround is to **avoid bounding the discontinuity directly** — either learn implicit/complementarity structure (ContactNets), or bound a *smoothed* surrogate and carry the smoothing error as a set (Li & Chou's smoothing-error reachable tubes, arXiv:2602.09368), or bound *per contact mode* and union.
- The most viable build for a solo inventor with a simulator-as-ground-truth: use MuJoCo/Drake/Dojo as the "true" g, learn ĝ on a **declared box operating domain in a fixed contact mode**, and certify ε with α,β-CROWN/auto_LiRPA on the discrepancy network, propagating it k steps with a discrete-Grönwall/reachability tube (CORA) evaluated through a signed-distance safety function. Declare and defend two assumptions: (1) the operating box stays inside one contact mode (or enumerate modes), and (2) the reference simulator *is* the ground truth for your guarantee (a sim-to-real gap remains uncertified). This is the only route that needs no unknown RKHS norm or Lipschitz constant.

## Key Findings

1. **There is no free lunch: every deterministic ε rests on an unfalsifiable-in-practice regularity assumption.** Kernel/RKHS bounds need a known bound on ‖g‖ in the RKHS; Lipschitz interpolation needs a known Lipschitz constant L; GP "deterministic" bounds need bounded-support noise plus an RKHS-norm bound. NN verification is the exception — it certifies a *computable* property of a *specific* network exactly (up to floating point), but it certifies the network, not the network's distance to the true dynamics unless you have a trusted reference model to diff against.

2. **Conformal prediction does NOT give a uniform bound and should be excluded from the two-sided/zero-miss guarantee.** It gives *marginal, distribution-free, high-probability coverage* over an exchangeable draw — a statement about average future draws, not a worst-case pointwise bound valid for all x in a domain. It is the statistical contrast case, not a substitute.

3. **Contact is the adversarial case for all of this.** Parmar–Halm–Posa (IROS 2021, arXiv:2103.15406) state it plainly: "We isolate the effects of contact's non-smoothness by varying the mechanical stiffness of a compliant contact simulator. Even for a simple system, we find that stiffness alone dramatically degrades training processes, generalization, and data-efficiency." As stiffness → rigid, the true map approaches a discontinuity, the effective Lipschitz constant blows up, and any bound of the form ε_k ≈ ε·(Σ Lⁱ) becomes vacuous over a horizon while the covering-number sample cost explodes.

4. **The single most relevant new paper is Li & Chou, "Certified Gradient-Based Contact-Rich Manipulation via Smoothing-Error Reachable Tubes" (arXiv:2602.09368, 2026).** It does NOT bound a learned model's error; it bounds the *smoothing error* between a convex-smoothed contact model f_κ and the true nonsmooth model f_0 analytically, and propagates it as a reachable tube. Its Theorem 1 gives a *tight, one-sided* deviation f_0 = f_κ − P⁻¹ Σ Jᵢᵀ (∂λ_κ,ᵢ/∂κ) κ wᵢ with wᵢ∈[1,2]. This is the template to imitate — but the theorem holds under a decoupled-contact assumption (JᵢP⁻¹Jⱼᵀ=0 for i≠j) and a quasistatic contact model.

5. **NN verification tooling is mature enough to be the backbone, at contact-relevant scales.** α,β-CROWN with auto_LiRPA supports bounding output ranges, Jacobians, and local Lipschitz constants over box/polytope inputs; GenBaB (TACAS 2025) extends branch-and-bound to general nonlinearities (tanh, sigmoid, trig — relevant for smooth contact surrogates); CORA does polynomial-zonotope reachability and closed-loop NN verification. Verified Safe RL (Wu, Zhang, Vorobeychik, NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2405.15994) is the proof-of-concept that this stack can certify *k-step* reachability for a *neural dynamics model* — but only for small ReLU nets and short horizons.

## Details

### Part 1 — Methods for uniform (non-statistical) error bounds

**(a) Lipschitz interpolation / nonlinear set-membership (Milanese–Novara lineage).**
- Origin: Milanese & Novara, "Set membership identification of nonlinear systems," *Automatica* 40(6):957–975, 2004. Assumptions: no functional-form assumption on g; instead a regularity assumption — g is Lipschitz (or has bounded gradient) with a *known* constant γ — plus *bounded* noise |v|≤σ. Output: the optimal (tightest) upper/lower bounds consistent with data + assumptions ("Feasible Function Set"), which are deterministic and pointwise.
- Guarantee type: deterministic, worst-case, valid for all x in the declared regressor domain W.
- Constants: the Lipschitz constant must be supplied. Modern successor Calliess et al., LACKI ("Lazily Adapted Constant Kinky Inference," *Automatica* 2020; arXiv:1701.00178) estimates L online from noisy data; Huang/Calliess (arXiv:2310.07039) prove convergence and note LACKI's estimate is only asymptotically the true best L (not finite-sample-safe).
- Cost: prediction is O(n) per query in n data points; cheap. Failure modes: **misspecified/underestimated L breaks the guarantee**; the estimated L from data is a lower bound on the truth, so a near-discontinuity yields a huge (or unbounded) L and a vacuous bound. Curse of dimensionality in covering the domain.

**(b) Kernel / RKHS deterministic error bounds (known RKHS-norm bound).**
- Maddalena, Scharnhorst & Jones, "Deterministic error bounds for kernel-based learning techniques under bounded noise," *Automatica* 134:109896, 2021 (arXiv:2008.04005). Assumptions: g lies in the RKHS of the chosen kernel with a *known bound* on ‖g‖_H; noise is bounded. Output: deterministic finite-sample pointwise error bound for kernel ridge regression and ε-SVR.
- Fiedler, Scherer & Trimpe, "Practical and Rigorous Uncertainty Bounds for Gaussian Process Regression," AAAI 2021 — frequentist, rigorous, explicitly evaluable and much less conservative than prior RKHS bounds.
- Newer: Lahr et al., "Optimal kernel regression bounds under energy-bounded noise," NeurIPS 2025 (arXiv:2505.22235) and the dual-function multi-output bound (arXiv:2603.16481) give tight, deterministic, GP-structured bounds under bounded/energy-bounded noise.
- Guarantee: deterministic if noise is bounded and ‖g‖_H bound is correct. Cost: O(n³) for the kernel inverse. Failure modes: **the RKHS-norm bound is unknown and hard to bound honestly**; ill-conditioned kernel matrices; contact functions are typically *not* in a smooth RKHS (a near-discontinuity has huge or infinite RKHS norm for smooth kernels).

**(c) Gaussian-process worst-case / deterministic bounds.**
- Srinivas et al. (GP-UCB) and Chowdhury & Gopalan give *high-probability* RKHS bounds using information gain Γ and an RKHS-norm bound B — conservative and needs Γ, B.
- Hashimoto et al. (2022) and Reed, Laurenti & Lahijanian, "Error Bounds for GP Regression Under Bounded Support Noise" (AAAI 2025; arXiv:2408.09033) give **deterministic** bounds |μ_D(x)−f(x)| ≤ β_T σ_D(x) when noise has bounded support σ_v — removing the information-gain term. Assumption still includes an RKHS-norm bound B.
- Failure modes: sub-Gaussian variants inflate σ_n and become conservative; δ→0 (certainty) needs infinite data; still needs B.

**(d) Scenario optimization (Campi & Garatti).**
- "The exact feasibility of randomized solutions of uncertain convex programs," *SIAM J. Optim.* 19(3):1211–1230, 2008. Gives an *a-priori, distribution-free* bound on the probability of constraint violation as a function of sample count N and number of decision variables d. Guarantee is **high-probability (risk ε at confidence 1−β)**, not deterministic. Sample cost grows with d (e.g., ~7,992 samples cited for 99.9% reliability at 95% confidence with one design variable). Useful for a *probabilistic* ε but not for a two-sided proven-not-measured guarantee.

**(e) Conformal prediction (statistical contrast).**
- Split CP (Vovk; Angelopoulos & Bates) gives finite-sample *marginal coverage* ≥1−δ for a prediction set, assuming only exchangeability of the calibration data. It is model-agnostic and cheap. **Why it is NOT a uniform bound:** coverage is averaged over the draw of (x,y); it does not certify |ĝ(x)−g(x)|≤ε for every x in a domain, and it degrades under distribution shift (contact regimes shift). Recent dynamics variants — PlanCP (NeurIPS 2023) for diffusion dynamics, invariant-measure CP (arXiv:2606.31607) which propagates a one-step conformal score via a Lipschitz argument over a horizon — are still probabilistic. Use CP only as a fallback/soft monitor, never as the input to the zero-miss guarantee.

**(f) Direct interval / NN verification of the discrepancy ĝ−g against a trusted reference.**
- This is the route that yields a *deterministic* ε without an unknown regularity constant, *provided* a trusted reference model g (e.g., a simulator, or a verified analytic contact model) exists. You build the network d(x)=ĝ(x)−g(x) (or bound ĝ and g separately) and use α,β-CROWN / auto_LiRPA / CORA to bound sup_x∈domain |d(x)|. Guarantee: deterministic over the declared box/polytope, exact up to the verifier's sound over-approximation and floating point. Cost: scales with network size and input dimension; branch-and-bound can blow up. Failure mode: the bound is only as true as the claim "g is the ground truth" — the sim-to-real gap is *not* certified by this step.

### Part 2 — Neural-network verification tooling

- **α,β-CROWN + auto_LiRPA** (Verified-Intelligence): GPU linear-bound-propagation (CROWN/LiRPA) plus branch-and-bound with per-neuron split (β) and cutting planes (GCP-CROWN). Per the project's own announcement (08/2025), "α,β-CROWN is the winner of VNN-COMP 2025 and is ranked top-1 in all scored benchmarks," and it has been the winning verifier five consecutive years (VNN-COMP 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025). The official VNN-COMP 2025 report (arXiv:2512.19007) places α,β-CROWN 1st, ahead of NeuralSAT (George Mason, 2nd), CORA (TU Munich, 4th), NNV (Vanderbilt, 5th), and nnenum (Stony Brook, 6th). It supports conv, residual, RNN, Transformer, and nonlinear ops (ReLU, tanh, sigmoid, trig, maxpool) and can bound Jacobians / Jacobian-vector products / **local Lipschitz constants** (Shi et al., NeurIPS 2022).
- **GenBaB** (Shi, Jin, Kolter, Jana, Hsieh, Zhang; TACAS 2025, arXiv:2405.21063): branch-and-bound for *general nonlinearities* — important because smooth contact surrogates use tanh/sigmoid/softplus, not just ReLU.
- **Interval Bound Propagation (IBP)** (Gowal et al. 2018): cheapest, loosest; sound. Good for a fast conservative first pass.
- **LipSDP** (Fazlyab et al.): semidefinite program for a global Lipschitz bound; tighter than spectral-norm products but SDP scaling limits it to modest networks. **LipBaB**: branch-and-bound for certified local Lipschitz. **Spectral-norm product** (∏‖Wᵢ‖₂): trivial, always valid, wildly conservative for deep nets.
- **CORA** (Althoff, TUM): polynomial-zonotope reachability, open- and closed-loop NN verification; non-convex output enclosures; placed 4th in VNN-COMP 2025 and participates in ARCH-COMP AINNCS.
- **Scalability limits (2026):** exact/BaB verification is practical for networks with thousands to low-millions of parameters and input boxes of modest effective dimension (the split cost is exponential in the number of "unstable" neurons / branched input dims). Deep compositions (the k-step rollout network) get looser as depth grows — Verified Safe RL explicitly notes bounds "become looser" as horizon K grows, motivating curriculum verification. High input dimension (contact state + control, e.g., 22-DOF in-hand) is at or beyond the practical edge for tight bounds without structure.

### Part 3 — Contact dynamics specifics

- **Parmar, Halm & Posa, "Fundamental Challenges in Deep Learning for Stiff Contact Dynamics," IROS 2021, pp. 5181–5188, arXiv:2103.15406.** They vary the mechanical stiffness of a compliant contact simulator and isolate its effect; in their words, "stiffness alone dramatically degrades training processes, generalization, and data-efficiency." Mechanistically, as stiffness→rigid, the one-step map's slope in the contact-normal direction grows without bound (nearly a step at impact), so (i) the target's Lipschitz constant blows up, and (ii) gradient-based learners see fast-changing/exploding gradients and cannot fit the steep region with bounded-width smooth networks. Direct consequence for certification: a global-Lipschitz-based ε or a covering argument is defeated by the very regime you care about.
- **ContactNets** (Pfrommer, Halm, Posa, CoRL 2020, arXiv:2009.11193): instead of learning force = f(state) end-to-end, learn *implicit* structure — inter-body signed distance and contact-frame Jacobians — with a complementarity/maximum-dissipation-inspired loss that avoids differentiating through the stiff dynamics. Trained on real cube-toss data: "After post-processing the original 750 tosses, the collected dataset contains 570 unique, high-quality tosses," with the cube tracked via AprilTags by "three 148 Hz PointGrey cameras using TagSLAM." This sidesteps the discontinuity by learning the *smooth generators* of a nonsmooth map.
- **Bianchini, Halm, Matni & Posa, "Generalization Bounded Implicit Learning of Nearly Discontinuous Functions," L4DC 2022, arXiv:2112.06881.** Directly relevant: derives *generalization bounds* for explicit vs. implicit formulations and shows prediction-error losses give loose bounds near steep slopes, whereas a **violation-based implicit loss** can tightly bound *graph distance* (a physically meaningful metric robust to input and output noise). This is the closest existing thing to a principled error measure for learned contact — but it is a *generalization* (statistical, in-expectation) bound, not a deterministic uniform ε.
- **LCP / convex contact formulations:** Anitescu–Potra formulate frictional rigid-body contact as a solvable LCP; Stewart–Trinkle time-stepping; Todorov's MuJoCo uses a convex relaxation; **Dojo** (Howell, Le Cleac'h et al., arXiv:2203.00806) uses a nonlinear complementarity problem with second-order friction cones solved by a custom interior-point method, exposing the central-path parameter as a *tunable smoothing knob* (high = smooth gradients, low = crisp contact) and giving smooth gradients via implicit differentiation. These convex/smoothed models are exactly the "trusted reference" or "smoothing surrogate" you can bound against.
- **Randomized smoothing of contact** (Suh, Pang, Tedrake, "Bundled Gradients through Contact via Randomized Smoothing," RA-L 2022, arXiv:2109.05143): convolves the nonsmooth dynamics with a probabilistic kernel to get a "bundled gradient" that is well-behaved. The smoothing *bias* (difference between smoothed and true dynamics) was characterized for optimization purposes but was **not** turned into a certified two-sided error bound until the reachable-tube work below. Global planning via local smoothing: Pang, Suh, Yang, Tedrake, *IEEE T-RO* 2023.

- **Deep dive — Li & Chou, arXiv:2602.09368 (2026), "Certified Gradient-Based Contact-Rich Manipulation via Smoothing-Error Reachable Tubes":**
  - *What is certified:* constraint satisfaction and goal reachability of the closed-loop trajectory under the **true nonsmooth hybrid dynamics**, while planning with the smoothed dynamics.
  - *How:* a differentiable simulator built on convex conic programs smooths both contact dynamics (log-barrier relaxation of the complementarity KKT condition with parameter κ) and contact geometry. Theorem 1 gives an analytic, *tight, one-sided* smoothing-error: f_0(x,u) = f_κ(x,u) − P(x)⁻¹ Σᵢ Jᵢ(x)ᵀ (∂λ_κ,ᵢ/∂κ) κ wᵢ, with wᵢ∈[1,2], written compactly as x⁺ = f_κ + E_κ(x,u) w. The tube is E_κ·[1,2]^{n_c}.
  - *Assumptions:* the tightness/decoupling result assumes JᵢP⁻¹Jⱼᵀ=0 for i≠j (contacts decoupled in the mass metric); a quasistatic contact model (velocities negligible); convex conic contact (single-valued solutions).
  - *Tools/scale:* differentiable convex conic layers + set-valued robust control / affine feedback (reachability-constrained System Level Synthesis). Demonstrated on planar pushing (2-DOF), bimanual non-prehensile (9-DOF), and dexterous in-hand reorientation (22-DOF), on a desktop (Intel Core Ultra 9, 64 GB), including hardware.
  - *Named open items:* the bound is for the *smoothing* discrepancy of a *model*, not for a *learned* network's error; the decoupled-contact assumption; quasistatic restriction; global optimality is out of scope (local minima only). This is your closest architectural analogue: replace "smoothing error of a convex surrogate" with "certified error of a learned surrogate" and reuse the tube machinery.

- **Deep dive — Wu, Zhang & Vorobeychik, arXiv:2405.15994 (NeurIPS 2024), "Verified Safe Reinforcement Learning for Neural Network Dynamic Models":**
  - *What is certified:* K-step *finite-horizon forward-reachability* safety — for all initial states in S₀ (a box), the k-step reachable set (over-approximated as a box by the verifier) does not intersect the unsafe set, for all k≤K. This is a *deterministic* guarantee for the *neural* dynamics model.
  - *Assumptions:* the transition function F is (given as) a **ReLU neural network** and is *known/exact for verification purposes* — i.e., they certify safety *of* the learned model, they do **not** certify that the learned model matches reality. Deterministic dynamics.
  - *Tools:* α,β-CROWN / auto_LiRPA for differentiable reachability over-approximation, wrapped in curriculum learning (grow K), incremental verification (reuse across gradient steps), memorization of near-unsafe regions, and a *collection* of initial-state-dependent controllers.
  - *Scale:* five control tasks (lane-following, obstacle avoidance, drone with fixed/moving obstacles); horizons up to an order of magnitude longer than baselines. Still small ReLU nets.
  - *Named open:* single universal controller is hard; bounds loosen with K; neural dynamics "clearly not universal." Crucially for you: this paper *assumes* the neural model is the system — exactly the gap your certified-ε step would fill.

### Part 4 — Error propagation through multi-step rollouts

- **Lipschitz / discrete-Grönwall growth:** if the true one-step map has Lipschitz constant L and the certified one-step error is ε, the k-step deviation is bounded by ε_k ≤ ε·(L^{k}−1)/(L−1) (for L≠1), i.e., geometric growth for L>1. For contact L≫1, so this is vacuous beyond a couple of steps. This is the fundamental reason horizon certification is hard and why the smoothing-tube and reachability communities use *set* propagation instead of scalar growth.
- **Reachable-set / tube tools:** zonotopes, polynomial zonotopes, sparse polynomial zonotopes (Kochdumper–Althoff), Taylor models (Flow*), and constrained/hybrid zonotopes; tools **CORA** (MATLAB, TUM), **JuliaReach** (ReachabilityAnalysis.jl), **Flow\*** (Taylor-model flowpipes), **dReach** (δ-reachability via dReal SMT). Validated/interval integration (e.g., RINO for NN-controlled ODEs). CORA and JuliaReach handle the wrapping effect via non-convex set representations.
- **Hybrid transitions / contact events:** guard crossings force *mode splitting* — each possible contact mode transition spawns a branch, so the number of reachable-set fragments can blow up combinatorially (mode-enumeration blowup). This is the practical killer for contact reachability over long horizons and many simultaneous contacts.
- **Propagating a certified one-step learned-model error into a k-step tube through a safety function:** the smoothing-tube paper (2602.09368) is the clearest instance of propagating a *one-step model discrepancy* into a k-step state/control tube and enforcing constraints; it evaluates constraints (including the unilateral contact structure) on the tube. Verified Safe RL propagates a box through the composed k-step network and intersects with the unsafe set. Neither yet takes a *learned-model certified ε* as the disturbance input — that composition is the open build.
- **Tractable dimensionality in 2026:** tight box/zonotope reachability with NN verifiers is comfortable for single-digit to low-tens state dimensions and short horizons (K on the order of tens of steps for small nets); the smoothing-tube approach reached 22-DOF but for a *model* (not a learned net) and with the quasistatic/decoupled assumptions and affine feedback keeping the tube tractable. General hybrid mode enumeration remains impractical past a handful of simultaneous contacts.

### Part 5 — Hybrid / piecewise structure as a workaround

- The dominant escape hatch is **per-contact-mode smoothness**: within a fixed contact mode (fixed active-set of contacts), the dynamics *are* smooth and Lipschitz with a moderate constant, so per-mode you can bound ε with any Part-1 method, then union over modes. Contact-implicit and mode-scheduled controllers, LCS (Linear Complementarity Systems) learning (Jin, Aydinoglu, Halm, Posa, "Learning Linear Complementarity Systems," L4DC 2022), and task-driven hybrid model reduction (arXiv:2211.16657) all exploit this.
- **What breaks at mode boundaries:** exactly where a certified per-mode bound is least valid — the switching surface (guard) is where the near-discontinuity lives, the Lipschitz constant across the guard is huge, and misclassifying the mode makes the bound false.
- **Does anyone certify the mode classifier itself?** This is thin. Learned mode boundaries / signed-distance zero-crossings can in principle be verified with the same NN verification tooling (bound the classifier's decision margin over a box), but I found no established method that *jointly* certifies (mode classifier correctness) ∧ (per-mode ε) ∧ (union soundness at the boundary) for learned contact. This is a genuine open gap and a plausible original contribution.

### Part 6 — Data and validation substrates for a solo inventor (no hardware)

- **DAIR/Posa cube-toss dataset:** 10 cm acrylic cube tossed onto wood, AprilTags at 148 Hz, **570 high-quality tosses** (from ~750 raw), released with ContactNets (github.com/DAIRLab/contact-nets). Real, contact-rich, with impacts + stiction + sliding. Widely reused as a benchmark (GNS, NeRD, etc.).
- **DAIR articulated-object toss dataset:** Bianchini, Halm & Posa, "Simultaneous Learning of Contact and Continuous Dynamics" (CoRL 2023, arXiv:2310.12054): "We automated the collection of over 500 tosses of a two-link articulated object using a Franka Panda 7-DOF robotic arm. We tracked the poses of the 2 links using TagSLAM." Harder continuous dynamics than a rigid cube.
- **MIT MCube planar pushing:** Yu, Bauza, Fazeli, Rodriguez, "More than a Million Ways to Be Pushed," IROS 2016 — high-fidelity pusher+object poses and interaction forces across surface/shape/contact-point/speed/acceleration. **Omnipush** (Bauza et al., 2019): 250 pushes × 250 objects, RGB-D + precise tracking, built to study model-learning generalization. MCube **planar-impact** dataset (Fazeli).
- **Simulator-as-ground-truth substrates (the key enabler for *deterministic* validation):** MuJoCo, Drake, Dojo. Because the simulator *is* the true g, you have a genuine reference model to diff against — so an NN verifier can certify |ĝ−g|≤ε honestly *for the simulator's dynamics*. Dojo is especially apt: its interior-point smoothing knob lets you generate matched smooth/crisp references, and it gives analytic gradients. NeRD (Neural Robot Dynamics, arXiv:2508.15755) demonstrates pretraining on 10k simulated cube tosses then fine-tuning on the 570 real ones — a template dataset pipeline.
- **End-to-end no-hardware validation blueprint:** (1) pick MuJoCo/Drake/Dojo task + fixed contact mode; (2) declare a box operating domain in (state, control); (3) learn ĝ; (4) certify ε = sup|ĝ−g| over the box with α,β-CROWN/auto_LiRPA (diff-network) — this ε is *deterministic w.r.t. the simulator*; (5) propagate ε as a disturbance through a k-step CORA/JuliaReach tube; (6) evaluate the tube through a signed-distance/clearance safety function; (7) feed ε into certabstain and empirically confirm the *proven* miss bound holds on held-out sim rollouts (empirical confirmation of a proven property, not the proof itself). The only uncertified link is sim→real.

### Part 7 — Prior art and patents

- **Closest patent: US 10,872,187 B2, "Verified runtime validation of verified cyber-physical system models" (ModelPlex)** — inventors André Platzer & Stefan Mitsch (CMU lineage), provisional 61/961,366 filed **Oct 11, 2013**, granted **Dec 22, 2020**; DARPA-funded (FA8750-12-2-0291). Synthesizes, by theorem proving (differential dynamic logic / KeYmaera), a *provably-correct* runtime monitor (model + controller + **prediction monitor**) from a verified model, and triggers **provably-safe fail-safe fallback** on non-compliance. It introduces an **ε-bounded plant with disturbance δ** and reasons about bounded model–reality deviation ("Unbounded deviation from the model between sample points just is unsafe"). Critical distinction: ε bounds a **hand-authored** hybrid model, not a **certified error of a learned/neural** function — there is no NN-error-certification step. This is the strongest obviousness reference against your composition, so your novelty position should lean on (i) the *learned-function* error certification and (ii) the *deterministic (non-statistical) miss-rate*.
- **Closest deterministic paper on a learned function:** "Approximate nonlinear MPC with safety-augmented neural networks" (arXiv:2304.09575) uses a *uniform deterministic* NN-approximation-error assumption ‖Π_NN−Π_RMPC‖∞≤ε feeding a runtime safety filter — but its safety proof holds *regardless* of ε (ε only affects acceptance frequency), so ε is not the load-bearing input to a miss bound.
- **Certified approximation-error primitives (no monitor composition):** Yang, Wang, Woolard, Xiang, "Guaranteed approximation error estimation of neural networks and model modification," *Neural Networks* 151:61–69, 2022 — certified worst-case uniform approximation error of a trained FF network over a compact set via Lipschitz + reachability. "Planning with Learned Dynamics: Guaranteed Safety and Reachability via Lipschitz Constants" (arXiv:2010.08993) bounds learned-vs-true error via error-Lipschitz over a trusted domain (framed with probabilistic coverage).
- **Statistical analogues (the target's *shape*, but conformal not deterministic):** "Learning Robot Safety from Sparse Human Feedback using Conformal Prediction" (arXiv:2501.04823) — a runtime safety monitor with a *guaranteed miss rate* via conformal calibration; FAIL-Detect (arXiv:2503.08558); Wabersich–Zeilinger predictive safety filter (arXiv:1812.05506) treats a uniform model-error bound as a *probabilistic* constraint; SAFED-RRT (arXiv:2212.06874) uses a "verified error bound" on learned dynamics but with statistical top-level guarantees.
- **RTA / safety-filter lineage:** Simplex (Seto/Sha 1998); Hobbs et al., "Runtime Assurance for Safety-Critical Systems," *IEEE Control Systems Magazine* 43(2):28–65, 2023; ASTM F3269; Collins/Boeing (Cofer et al., NASA NFM 2020) and NASA Langley RTA frameworks — these give deterministic safety *assuming an accurate model or valid CBF*; they do not generally take a *certified learned-model ε* as the explicit input to a miss bound. Papers exist; confirmed patents on the specific composition were not found.
- **Verdict:** the exact two-part composition — a *certified deterministic ε on a learned dynamics/safety function* as the explicit driver of a *proven (deterministic) bounded/zero miss rate* in an abstention layer — appears **unclaimed as an integrated unit**. It is, however, a natural combination of known blocks, and ModelPlex is the reference most likely to be cited against novelty/obviousness. (Patent search was Google-Patents-snippet-based plus one full-text pull, not an exhaustive CPC-class docket search; a formal FTO search over G05B/G06N + "run-time assurance"/"safety filter" for Boeing, Collins, NASA, Nvidia, and Toyota Research is recommended before relying on novelty.)

### Part 8 — Named open problems and the literature's own workarounds

- **Discontinuity breaks global Lipschitz:** the core obstruction; named in Parmar–Halm–Posa and Bianchini et al. Workaround proposed by the literature: *implicit / violation-based losses* (ContactNets, Bianchini) and *per-mode* Lipschitz.
- **Covering-argument curse of dimensionality:** deterministic Lipschitz-interpolation and RKHS covering bounds need sample/verification cost exponential in effective input dimension; contact state+control is high-dimensional. Workaround: exploit structure (quasistatic reduction, decoupled contacts, affine feedback tubes) as in 2602.09368.
- **Conservatism → vacuous bounds:** naive spectral-norm or global-Lipschitz bounds and long-horizon Grönwall growth become vacuous. Workaround: branch-and-bound NN verification (α,β-CROWN, GenBaB), local/state-dependent Lipschitz (Shi et al.), set propagation instead of scalar growth.
- **Unknown true Lipschitz constant / RKHS norm:** the assumption you can't honestly verify. Workaround: LACKI online estimation (only asymptotically safe); or *avoid it entirely* by using NN verification against a trusted reference (no regularity constant needed).
- **Certifying the mode classifier and boundary behavior:** open; no established joint certification. A candidate original contribution.
- **Certified smoothing:** the newest direction (2602.09368) — certify the *smoothing* error analytically and propagate it; open question is extending it from convex-surrogate smoothing error to *learned-model* error.

## Recommendations

**Route A (recommended first build): NN verification of the discrepancy against a simulator reference.**
- Do this: freeze a contact task in MuJoCo/Drake/Dojo, restrict to a declared box operating domain within a single contact mode, learn ĝ as a small tanh/ReLU net, build d=ĝ−g (or bound ĝ and g's verified surrogate separately), and certify ε=sup|d| with α,β-CROWN/auto_LiRPA (use GenBaB if smooth activations). Propagate ε with CORA/JuliaReach as a disturbance tube, evaluate through signed distance, feed ε to certabstain.
- **Assumption you must declare and defend:** (1) the operating box lies inside one contact mode (or you enumerate a finite, verified mode set), and (2) the simulator is the ground truth — your guarantee is "certified w.r.t. the reference model," and the sim→real gap is explicitly *out of certification* (optionally bounded separately by conformal/scenario methods as a *soft* layer).
- **Why first:** it needs no unknown RKHS norm or Lipschitz constant; the tooling is mature (α,β-CROWN won VNN-COMP 2025, top-1 in all scored benchmarks); and it mirrors the two proven templates (2405.15994 for k-step neural reachability, 2602.09368 for tube propagation and safety-function evaluation).
- **Benchmark that would change the plan:** if the verified ε over your box is already vacuous (tube engulfs the clearance) at horizon k≈5–10 for a low-DOF task, the network/domain is too expressive/large — shrink the box, distill to a smaller net, or move to Route C.

**Route B (parallel, for the smooth-surrogate path): imitate the smoothing-error tube.**
- If you prefer a physics surrogate over a black-box net, adopt the 2602.09368 construction directly: use Dojo/convex-smoothed contact as f_κ, carry E_κ·[1,2] as the certified one-step error set, and if you *also* learn a residual, certify the residual with Route A tooling and add it to the tube.
- **Assumption to defend:** decoupled contacts (JᵢP⁻¹Jⱼᵀ≈0) and quasistatic regime; state these as domain-of-validity conditions on certabstain.

**Route C (fallback when A/B give vacuous ε): per-mode kernel/Lipschitz bounds.**
- Within a single fixed mode, use Maddalena–Scharnhorst–Jones KRR bounds or Fiedler–Scherer–Trimpe GP bounds with a *declared* RKHS-norm bound, or LACKI with an estimated L. Union over an enumerated mode set.
- **Assumption to defend (hard):** a *correct* RKHS-norm/Lipschitz bound. Because this is the weakest link, use Route C only per-mode (where the constant is moderate) and never across a guard.

**Staged plan:** (1) Prototype Route A on a 2-DOF planar push in Dojo/MuJoCo, K≈10; measure certified ε and tube tightness against clearance. (2) If tight, scale DOF and horizon and add mode enumeration; if not, shrink domain / distill. (3) Add a conformal *soft* monitor for the sim→real gap as a second, clearly-labeled statistical layer — never conflated with the deterministic guarantee. (4) For IP, draft claims emphasizing the *learned-function certified ε* and *deterministic miss bound*, and design around ModelPlex (US 10,872,187).

## Caveats
- **arXiv:2602.09368, 2603.16481, 2606.31607 and similar 2026 IDs are recent preprints**, not all peer-reviewed; treat leading-edge claims as provisional. The 2602.09368 PDF text layer was unreadable; details here are from the arXiv HTML body and the authors' project descriptions.
- **"Certified" in the contact-manipulation papers means certified w.r.t. a declared model** (smoothed vs. nonsmooth, or the neural model treated as truth), **not** certified w.r.t. physical reality. Verified Safe RL explicitly assumes the neural dynamics *is* the system; the smoothing-tube paper certifies against the nonsmooth *model*. Your certabstain guarantee inherits exactly this "true relative to the reference" character unless you separately (and only statistically) bound sim→real.
- **The patent search is not exhaustive** (Google-Patents snippets plus one full-text pull); a formal FTO/novelty opinion needs a CPC-class docket search. The novelty verdict ("unclaimed as an integrated unit") is a research finding, not a legal opinion. The ModelPlex application/grant dates (provisional Oct 11, 2013; granted Dec 22, 2020) were reported by the subagent from the USPTO full text and should be re-confirmed against the official register.
- **Deterministic vs. high-probability vs. empirical** were kept distinct throughout: NN-verification / interval / Lipschitz-interpolation / RKHS-under-bounded-noise = deterministic (given their assumptions); GP-UCB / Chowdhury–Gopalan / scenario / conformal = high-probability; dataset rollouts = empirical. Only the deterministic class can feed a "proven not measured" zero-miss guarantee.
- **Unknown-constant risk dominates:** the honest bottleneck for contact is not tooling but the *assumption* — an unknown/underestimated Lipschitz or RKHS-norm constant silently invalidates Route C; Route A's reference-model assumption is at least explicit and testable in simulation.
